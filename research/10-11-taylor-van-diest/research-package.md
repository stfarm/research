# 10-11 — Taylor Van Diest (Armstrong, British Columbia, 2011)

Research package. Not a script. Researched 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-01.

> ## LEGAL STATUS — READ FIRST
> - Matthew Foerster was convicted of **first-degree murder** by a jury in April 2014.
> - That conviction was **set aside** by the B.C. Court of Appeal on **March 7, 2017**, which ordered a new trial over errors in the judge's jury instructions.
> - Before a retrial, Foerster **pleaded guilty to second-degree murder** (March 12, 2018).
> - **Final sentence (June 2018):** life imprisonment, no parole eligibility for **17 years** (from his April 4, 2012 arrest). Parole eligibility: **April 4, 2029**. Faint-hope application possible from **April 4, 2027** (reported).
> - **The operative conviction is second-degree murder.** Do not present the 2014 first-degree conviction or its 25-year parole bar as current.

## Case summary

- On Halloween, **October 31, 2011**, Taylor Van Diest, 18, dressed as a zombie, set out on foot around 6 p.m. in Armstrong, B.C. to meet friends for Halloween. She took a shortcut along the railway tracks. [10-11-S002, 10-11-S013, 10-11-S008]
- At **6:01 p.m.**, she texted a friend that she was "being creeped". It was her last message. [10-11-S008, 10-11-S009]
- She was found badly injured and unconscious near the tracks around 8:30 p.m. by searchers, including family and friends. She died on **November 1, 2011**, at Kelowna General Hospital. [10-11-S002, 10-11-S003, 10-11-S008]
- She and her attacker were strangers. [10-11-S008]
- After a five-month investigation, RCMP linked DNA from the case to an unsolved 2005 sexual assault in Kelowna. On **April 4, 2012**, they arrested **Matthew Stephen Foerster**, 26, formerly of Cherryville, in Ontario. His father, **Stephen Roy Foerster**, was also arrested. [10-11-S001, 10-11-S003]
- Matthew Foerster was convicted of first-degree murder in April 2014. The B.C. Court of Appeal ordered a new trial in 2017. He then pleaded guilty to second-degree murder in 2018 and was sentenced to life with no parole for 17 years. [10-11-S003, 10-11-S006, 10-11-S007, 10-11-S008]

## Victim profile

- **Taylor Jade Van Diest**, 18, of Armstrong, B.C. (North Okanagan). [10-11-S002, 10-11-S014]
- **Twin sister: Kirstie.** Both graduated from Pleasant Valley Secondary School in Armstrong in June 2011. [10-11-S014]
- Parents: **Marie** and **Raymond** Van Diest. [10-11-S007, 10-11-S008]
- She prepared her Halloween zombie makeup at home around midday on October 31 and wore a tan-coloured jacket. [10-11-S002]
- Her mother at sentencing: "The hardest thing I ever had to do as a mother was to tell her twin sister Taylor is gone. There will always remain an empty chair." [10-11-S008]
- A memorial trail, the **Taylor Jade Van Diest Memorial Trail** (~185 m, linking Rosedale Avenue to Pleasant Valley Road), was built by the family with the city and the Kelowna Pacific Railway. Community volunteers maintained it in April 2026. [10-11-S014, 10-11-S016]

## Investigation

- **Discovery:** Searchers found Taylor near the railway tracks about 2.5 hours after her last text. [10-11-S003, 10-11-S008]
- **Text message:** "Being creeped" at 6:01 p.m. established the time of the attack and that she felt followed. Its legal use later became an appeal issue. [10-11-S006, 10-11-S008]
- **Public appeal:** Police released costume photos to reconstruct her last hours. [10-11-S002, 10-11-S012]
- **Breakthrough (DNA):** DNA recovered in the Van Diest investigation matched DNA from the unsolved April 2005 Kelowna sexual assault. That link pointed investigators to Foerster. Secondary sources say the DNA came from under her fingernails after she scratched her attacker. **Fingernail detail unverified. Say "DNA evidence" only.** [10-11-S001, 10-11-S003]
- **Flight / concealment:** Foerster was living and working in Ontario under false identification bought by his father. [10-11-S004]
- **Specialized techniques:** RCMP cited "specialized investigative techniques", ViCLAS analysts, and behavioural profilers. **Do not speculate about an undercover or "Mr. Big" operation.** None confirmed in sources reviewed. [10-11-S001]

### Plain-language explanation of the legal change

1. **2014:** A jury found Foerster guilty of first-degree murder. In Canada that means life with no parole for 25 years. The jury found he killed her while intending to sexually assault her. [10-11-S003]
2. **2017:** The B.C. Court of Appeal found the trial judge gave the jury two flawed instructions:
   - how they could use his disposing of the shoelace and flashlight;
   - how they could use Taylor's "being creeped" text.

   Because those errors could have affected the verdict, the court ordered a new trial. **This was not a finding that he was innocent.** [10-11-S006]
3. **2018:** Instead of a second trial, Foerster pleaded guilty to second-degree murder. Second-degree murder also carries a mandatory life sentence. The judge set parole ineligibility at 17 years, jointly recommended by Crown and defence. [10-11-S007, 10-11-S008]
4. **Family view:** Marie Van Diest: "To us, he's always going to be guilty of first degree." [10-11-S007]

**Faint-hope note:** The faint-hope clause (Criminal Code s. 745.6) was repealed for offences committed on or after **Dec 2, 2011**. Taylor's murder (Oct 31, 2011) predates that, which is why the 2027 application date is reported. This is general legal knowledge; **confirm with a legal source before scripting.** Faint-hope is a court-and-jury review process, not a parole board decision. Black Press says it is "up to the parole board", which is imprecise.

- **Central question:** Who followed Taylor on the tracks, and how do you find a stranger?
