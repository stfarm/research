# 10-31 — Teresa Halbach (Manitowoc County, Wisconsin, Halloween 2005)

Research package. Not a script. Researched 2026-10-10. **Halloween finale slot.**

> ## READ FIRST
> - **Both convictions stand.** Steven Avery: first-degree intentional homicide + felon in possession (2007), life without parole. Brendan Dassey: first-degree intentional homicide, second-degree sexual assault, mutilation of a corpse (2007), life with parole eligibility in 2048. [10-31-S001, 10-31-S003, 10-31-S004]
> - **Avery was acquitted of mutilating a corpse.** [10-31-S008]
> - **Live as of Sept 2026:** both men filed commutation applications. A Manitowoc judge called Avery's "entirely inappropriate"; the board meets Oct 23, 2026, and neither is on the agenda. **Re-check on Oct 30–31.** [10-31-S004]
> - Defamation risk: claims that police planted evidence and alternate-suspect theories are **allegations** rejected by courts (S001 *Denny* ruling; S004 Jan 2025 / May 2025 rulings). Never name an alternate suspect as the killer.
> - Do not describe the sexual-assault allegations or the remains beyond "burned bone fragments".

## Halloween connection

**Classification: A** — disappeared and (per the state's case) killed on **October 31**; witnesses described a **large bonfire on Halloween night** in the burn pit where her remains were found.

- "Teresa Halbach disappeared on Halloween Day, 2005." — 7th Circuit. [10-31-S002]
- Avery called Auto Trader **the morning of Oct 31**; Halbach confirmed just before 2:30 p.m. she was heading there. [10-31-S001, 10-31-S002]
- "Multiple witnesses reported seeing a large bonfire in the burn pit outside of Avery's residence on October 31." A witness put Dassey at the bonfire with Avery around 7:30–7:45 p.m. [10-31-S002]
- **Not sourced:** trick-or-treaters, costumes, Halloween decorations at the yard. Do not add them.

## Story engine (angles, not script)

**Opening hooks (factual):**
1. On Halloween 2005, a 25-year-old photographer took her last assignment: a minivan at a salvage yard. The man who requested her asked for "the same girl who had been out here before." [S002]
2. That night, witnesses saw a huge bonfire behind his trailer. [S002]
3. The man had just walked out of prison as an exonerated innocent — and was suing the county for $36 million. [S008]

**Suspense beats:** voicemail full → missing report three days later → volunteer searchers walking a 4,000-car yard → RAV4 found under branches with plates off → bones in the burn pit → key in the bedroom → a 16-year-old nephew's confession.

**Reveal / question:** Two men are convicted; one was the country's most famous exoneree. Twenty years and two documentaries later, every court has upheld the verdicts — and in September 2026 both asked the governor to cut their sentences. [S004]

**Victim focus (recommended):** She was a photographer who documented other people's lives; her family has stayed largely out of the spotlight (no family statements found in 2025–2026 coverage, S007).

## Distinguishing details

- Phone call asking for "the same girl" (S002).

- 2026: commutation applications; judge's "entirely inappropriate" (S004).

## Disputed claims / myths / avoid

- **Never** say Avery was framed, or that evidence was planted — allegations only.
- **Never** name Bobby Dassey, Scott Tadych, Ryan Hillegas or any other person as a suspect. Courts excluded/rejected third-party claims (S001, S004).
- **Never** say Dassey "now says Avery did it" — unverified creator claim (S005 shows no such statement).
- Do not say "Avery was convicted of mutilation".
- Do not state bonfire flame heights (testimony later disputed).
- Do not say "killed on Halloween night" as a court finding — say "disappeared on Halloween" and "never seen after that afternoon".
- Do not use "Steven Avery was released" — he has not been.
