# Script facts — 10-17 Preston Lord

Verified facts only. Source IDs refer to `source-log.md`. **Active case. Status as of 2026-10-01. Re-check before publishing.**

# Essential facts

- **Preston Lord**, 16, of Queen Creek, Arizona. Parents: Nick Lord and Autumn Curiel. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S013]
- **Saturday, October 28, 2023:** Preston was at a Halloween party at a home in Queen Creek. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S002]
- Outside the party, he was beaten by a group. Police arrived in about three minutes and found him in the road. [10-17-S002]
- He died two days later, on **October 30, 2023**. The medical examiner ruled it a homicide caused by multiple blunt-force injuries. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S007]

# Timeline

- Nov 2023 – Feb 2024: four months without arrests; the community pushes for answers. [10-17-S002]
- Feb 2024: an investigative grand jury hears evidence for five weeks. [10-17-S001]
- **March 2024:** seven young men, aged about 17 to 20, are indicted. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S002]
- March 2025: one of them, **William Owen Hines**, pleads guilty to manslaughter. [10-17-S005]
- **May 2, 2025:** Hines is sentenced to 12 years for Preston's death (17 in total with an unrelated drunk-driving crash). [10-17-S004]
- **May 12, 2025:** Arizona's governor signs **Preston's Law**, making group "swarming" assaults a felony. [10-17-S013]
- Sep 2026: the judge sets the first trial, for defendant **Jacob Meisner**, for **November 3, 2026**. [10-17-S006]

# Evidence reveal

- Prosecutors said they reviewed about **600 videos** and a **2,000-page** police report. [10-17-S001]
- Hines admitted kicking Preston at least once. [10-17-S006]
- The other six defendants deny the charges. Meisner's lawyers say none of 13 eyewitnesses identified him. [10-17-S006, 10-17-S010]

# Ending

- **One conviction:** Hines, manslaughter (by plea). [10-17-S004]
- **Six defendants presumed innocent**, charged with first-degree murder and kidnapping (three also with aggravated robbery). All pleaded not guilty. [10-17-S006]
- First trial: Nov 3, 2026. No dates yet for the other five. [10-17-S006]
- Preston's parents settled a wrongful-death lawsuit with Hines, one other defendant, and the homeowners who hosted the party. [10-17-S012]
- County Attorney Rachel Mitchell (Mar 2024): "We have taken a massive step forward in the quest for justice for Preston." [10-17-S001]

- Never say "seven people were convicted" or "suspects were sentenced". One was, for manslaughter.
- Never say Hines was convicted of murder. It was manslaughter.
- Never present the police-report account (a dispute over a gold chain) as fact. Attribute it, or omit it.
