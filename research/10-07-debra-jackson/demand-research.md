## What the demand picture actually shows

1. **This case is broadcaster-led, not creator-led** — the inverse of the other
   two in this batch. Six of the ten located videos are local TV news packages
   (KXAN, Fox 7 Austin, the *Statesman*, Abilene-market stations). Independent
   creator coverage is comparatively thin at four videos.
2. **Recognition is high but attached to the wrong name.** The case's fame runs
   through **Henry Lee Lucas** — a Netflix series, decades of books and
   journalism, *America's Most Wanted* twice. Debra Jackson is famous as the
   victim in someone else's story. A Jackson-centred episode is genuinely
   available ground.
3. **Packaging in the creator niche is "identified after 40 years" / "SOLVED
   after 40 years."** The second formulation is factually wrong and is the exact
   error the brief forbids. Getting it right — identified, not solved — is both
   more accurate and a sharper hook, because the unresolved murder is the more
   interesting fact.
4. **The Halloween framing is already the press's own.** KXAN's October 2025
   headline is built on "found dead on Halloween," so leaning on the
   discovery-date connection follows the local reporting rather than stretching
   it.
5. **There is a live news peg.** The October 2025 anniversary coverage confirms
   an open investigation and a specific public appeal — witnesses to her
   whereabouts between 1977 and 1979. That is current, actionable and absent
   from every creator treatment located.
6. **Angles other creators emphasise:** the Jane Doe mystery and the
   identification; Lucas as the serial killer; the "orange socks" detail as a
   hook.
7. **Under-served and available:** the **1978 Amarillo employment** (the only
   documented point in her two missing years); the **two-or-more male DNA
   contributors** from 1979 evidence; the **headstone changing from
   "unidentified woman" to her name**; and the fact that her **sister recognised
   a forensic sketch on television** — the human mechanism that actually named
   her, which the genealogy-focused coverage tends to bury.
