# 10-10 — Martha Moxley (Greenwich, Connecticut, 1975)

Research package. Not a script. Researched 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-01.

> ## LEGAL STATUS — READ FIRST
> - Michael Skakel was **convicted** of Martha Moxley's murder in 2002.
> - That conviction was **vacated** by the Connecticut Supreme Court on **May 4, 2018** (4–3), on ineffective assistance of counsel. The U.S. Supreme Court declined review on **January 7, 2019**.
> - On **October 30, 2020**, Chief State's Attorney **Richard J. Colangelo Jr.** announced the state **would not retry** Skakel, saying it could not prove the case beyond a reasonable doubt.
> - Skakel **stands unconvicted** and has always maintained his innocence. **No one stands convicted of Martha's murder.** The homicide is **legally unresolved**.
> - **Never state or imply that Michael Skakel (or anyone else) killed Martha Moxley.**
> - The 2018 decision was **not** a finding of innocence. The court said the trial "cannot be relied on as having produced a just result". Do not say he was "exonerated" or "proven innocent" either.

## Case summary

- Martha Moxley, 15, left her family's home in the Belle Haven section of Greenwich on the evening of **October 30, 1975** to celebrate "mischief night", the night before Halloween, with friends. [10-10-S001]
- She was last seen by friends around **9:30 p.m.** in the Skakel family's driveway, across the street from her home, with 17-year-old Thomas Skakel. [10-10-S001]
- Her mother reported her missing to Greenwich police at **3:48 a.m.** on October 31. [10-10-S001]
- Her body was discovered in the **early afternoon of October 31, 1975**, under a pine tree behind her parents' home. She had been beaten with a golf club later determined to come from a set belonging to Michael and Thomas Skakel's late mother. [10-10-S001]
- Thomas Skakel was for years the prime suspect. The family's tutor, Kenneth Littleton, was also investigated. The case went cold. [10-10-S001]
- In the 1990s, private-investigator files, journalism, and books renewed attention. A grand jury was convened in 1998. Michael Skakel, also 15 in 1975, surrendered in January 2000. [10-10-S001, 10-10-S005]
- A jury convicted Michael Skakel on **June 7, 2002**. He was sentenced to 20 years to life on **August 29, 2002**. [10-10-S001, 10-10-S016]
- After habeas litigation, the Connecticut Supreme Court vacated the conviction in 2018, and prosecutors declined to retry in 2020. [10-10-S001, 10-10-S004, 10-10-S015]

## Victim profile

- **Martha Elizabeth Moxley**, born August 16, 1960 (tertiary source). [10-10-S013]
- Daughter of **Dorthy** and **David Moxley**. Her older brother is **John Moxley**. The court opinion spells her mother's name "Dorothy". Her own spelling, used in her obituary coverage, is **Dorthy**. [10-10-S001, 10-10-S007]
- The family moved from Piedmont, California, to Belle Haven in Greenwich in the summer of 1974. Martha was a sophomore at Greenwich High School (secondary sources). [10-10-S014]
- Her mother testified that Martha was "extremely reliable" about coming home on time. [10-10-S001]
- Martha kept a diary in the months before her death, which later received wide media attention (secondary). [10-10-S014]
- She is buried at Putnam Cemetery, Greenwich (Commons photo description). [10-10-S017]
- **Dorthy Moxley** spent decades pressing for the case to be solved. She died **December 24, 2024**, at 92, at her home in Summit, New Jersey, after the flu. [10-10-S007]

## Investigation

- **Discovery:** Body found on Oct 31, 1975, under a pine tree behind the Moxley home. Blood spatter showed she was first attacked in or near her driveway. [10-10-S001]
- **Weapon:** A golf club, a 6-iron per press reports, from a set that belonged to the Skakels' late mother. It was broken during the attack. [10-10-S001, 10-10-S005]
- **Time of death:** Contested. Dr. Joseph Jachimczyk, retained by Greenwich police, estimated about 10 p.m. The state's chief medical examiner, H. Wayne Carver II, testified to a window of 9:30 p.m. to 12–1 a.m. [10-10-S001]
- **Early suspects:**
  - Thomas Skakel, the last person seen with her. Police were refused a warrant in 1976. [10-10-S001]
  - Kenneth Littleton, the tutor. Per the court, "after considerable investigation, he was exonerated". [10-10-S001]
- **Revival:** Sutton Associates (1991+) recorded both brothers changing their 1975 accounts. Leaked files reached Dunne, Levitt, and Fuhrman. [10-10-S001]
- **Turning point toward charges:** After 1995–1998 publicity, witnesses came forward claiming Michael Skakel had made incriminating statements, including Gregory Coleman and John Higgins from the Elan School. The court called their testimony "the cornerstone of the state's case". [10-10-S001]
- **Forensics:** The 2018 court said the conviction rested on a case "devoid of any forensic evidence or eyewitness testimony linking the petitioner to the crime". [10-10-S001]

## Halloween connection

**Classification: C — major discovery occurred on October 31.** (She was out for Mischief Night on Oct 30.)

- Martha was last seen on **October 30, 1975**, while out celebrating **mischief night**, which the court defines as "the night before Halloween". [10-10-S001]
- Her body was found on **Halloween, October 31, 1975**. [10-10-S001]
- Medical testimony and the defense theory place the attack on the night of October 30, not on Halloween. **Do not say she was killed on Halloween.**
- Secondary note: Oct 30 is also the anniversary date of the 2020 no-retrial announcement.

### Separation of claims

- **Court findings:**
  - 2018: trial counsel was constitutionally deficient for failing to identify and call Ossorio. The state's case was "far from overwhelming" and "devoid of any forensic evidence or eyewitness testimony linking the petitioner to the crime".
  - 2002: jury verdict (now vacated). [10-10-S001]
- **Prosecution arguments (trial):**
  - Skakel's alibi was a family construct.
  - His tree story was meant to explain potential evidence near the body.
  - The Elan School confessions were genuine.
  - The barking dog suggested Michael was present. [10-10-S001]
- **Defense arguments:**
  - Time of death was 9:30–10 p.m., while Michael was at the Terrien home.
  - No forensic link.
  - Coleman was unreliable.
  - Thomas Skakel was last seen with her. [10-10-S001]
- **Allegations (unadjudicated):**
  - Third-party theories naming other men, rejected as grounds for a new trial in 2010.
  - Skakel's civil claims against Garr and the town.
  - Claims in books (Fuhrman, RFK Jr.'s *Framed*). [10-10-S001, 10-10-S010, 10-10-S013]

## Story structure

- **Strongest factual hook:** On the night before Halloween in 1975, a 15-year-old went out for mischief night in one of America's wealthiest neighborhoods and never came home. She was found the next afternoon under a pine tree in her own backyard. [10-10-S001]
- **Central question:** How does a murder with a known weapon, a known timeline, and a short list of neighbors produce a conviction that is later thrown out, and leave the case unresolved 50 years on?
- **Turning point:** The 2018 Connecticut Supreme Court ruling: a disinterested alibi witness (Denis Ossorio) was never called. [10-10-S001]
- **Ending:** No retrial (2020). Martha's mother died in 2024 without a resolution. In 2025, Skakel spoke publicly for the first time, and his civil suits against the town and investigator are pending. **The case remains unresolved.** [10-10-S005, 10-10-S007, 10-10-S009, 10-10-S011]
