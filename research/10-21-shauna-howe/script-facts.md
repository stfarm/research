# Script facts — 10-21 Shauna Howe

Verified facts only. Source IDs refer to `source-log.md`.

# Essential facts

- **Shauna Howe**, 11, a Girl Scout in Oil City, Pennsylvania. Her favourite colour was purple. [10-21-S006]
- **Tuesday, October 27, 1992, about 8 p.m.:** she walked home from a Girl Scout Halloween party. A friend walked part of the way with her. [10-21-S007]
- Two blocks from home, at West First and Reed streets, a man grabbed her. A witness saw it. [10-21-S007, 10-21-S008]
- Three days later, on **October 30**, her body was found beneath a railroad trestle at Coulter's Hole, about eight miles away. [10-21-S003, 10-21-S005]

# Timeline

- 1992–2002: no arrest. The FBI and state police helped; the case went cold. [10-21-S008]
- 1993–2007: Oil City held trick-or-treat in daylight only, with police patrolling. [10-21-S003]
- 2002: DNA from evidence kept since 1992 matched **James O'Brien**. [10-21-S007, 10-21-S008]
- July 2004: James O'Brien, his brother **Timothy**, and **Ted Walker** were arrested. [10-21-S006, 10-21-S008]
- 2005: Walker pleaded guilty and testified. In October 2005 a jury convicted both brothers. [10-21-S004, 10-21-S005]
- April 2006: both brothers sentenced to life plus 15 to 30 years. [10-21-S004]
- Sept 2008: after a 10-year-old's petition, the city brought back evening trick-or-treating. [10-21-S003]
- Nov 6, 2022: Walker died in prison. [10-21-S005]

# Evidence reveal

- Investigators had preserved biological evidence from Shauna and her costume. That is what made the DNA match possible a decade later. [10-21-S007]
- Walker testified he grabbed Shauna and handed her to Timothy O'Brien, who was waiting around the corner, while James drove. [10-21-S001]
- A witness recognised Walker on television in 2002. [10-21-S007]

# Ending

- James and Timothy O'Brien: life in prison (plus 15 to 30 years). [10-21-S004]
- Ted Walker: 20 to 40 years; died in prison in 2022. [10-21-S005]
- The judge at sentencing: "While Shauna Howe was a member of the Girl Scouts enriching the lives of senior citizens at a convalescent home, you were lurking in the shadows plotting to ruin her life." [10-21-S004]
- Shauna's mother, 30 years later: "I want people to remember Shauna." [10-21-S006]
- In 2008, fifth-grader Elizabeth Roess gathered 175 signatures. City council voted unanimously to bring trick-or-treating back after dark. [10-21-S003]

- Never say the O'Briens were convicted in 2006. The verdict was October 2005; sentencing was April 2006.
