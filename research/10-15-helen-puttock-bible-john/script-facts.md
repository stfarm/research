# Script facts — 10-15 Helen Puttock / "Bible John"

Verified facts only. Source IDs refer to `source-log.md`.

# Essential facts

- **Helen Puttock**, 29, lived on Earl Street, Scotstoun, Glasgow, with her two young sons. Her husband George was a soldier. [10-15-S002, 10-15-S005]
- **Thursday, October 30, 1969:** Helen went dancing at the **Barrowland Ballroom** with her sister **Jeannie**. George, just home on leave, did not want her to go. [10-15-S005, 10-15-S011]
- The sisters met two men who both said they were called John. One left for a bus. The other shared their taxi home. [10-15-S001, 10-15-S002]
- Jeannie got out first, in Knightswood. Helen stayed in the taxi with the man. [10-15-S002]
- The next morning, **Friday, October 31, 1969**, a man walking his dog found Helen's body behind her own tenement. She had been beaten, sexually assaulted, and strangled. [10-15-S002, 10-15-S011]

# Timeline

- 1968 and Aug 1969: two other Glasgow women, **Patricia Docker** and **Jemima MacDonald**, were murdered after nights out dancing. Both cases are unresolved. [10-15-S001, 10-15-S013]
- Oct 31, 1969: Helen found. [10-15-S011]
- 1969–70: police release a photofit and a painted portrait of the man in the taxi. [10-15-S001]
- 1996: police exhume an early suspect, John Irvine McInnes, to test his DNA. The Crown says there is no evidence he was the source, and he is formally cleared. [10-15-S005, 10-15-S008]
- 2010: Jeannie dies, aged 74. [10-15-S007]
- 2022: George Puttock dies. A BBC podcast alleges a police cover-up. [10-15-S003, 10-15-S005]
- Oct 2023: Police Scotland says it is assessing the podcast's claims with the Crown Office. [10-15-S003]

# Evidence reveal

- Jeannie described the man: about 25 to 30, around six feet tall, reddish or sandy hair, blue-grey eyes, well dressed, with overlapping front teeth. [10-15-S001]
- In the taxi he quoted the Bible and spoke disapprovingly of women at dance halls. The press called him **"Bible John"**. [10-15-S001, 10-15-S002]
- Police preserved a semen stain from Helen's stockings. Decades later, DNA from it was used to rule suspects **out**. [10-15-S005, 10-15-S008]
- Jeannie viewed hundreds of identity parades over the years and never identified anyone. [10-15-S001]

# Ending

- No one has ever been charged with Helen's murder. [10-15-S005]
- Police Scotland: "Unresolved murder cases in Scotland are never closed." [10-15-S005]
- George, in his final interview: "I have to accept that I will probably never know who killed my Helen and the other women." [10-15-S005]
- Her son David: "I wish that police could look at the murders again and try to get some answers for the families." [10-15-S006]

# Important names

- Helen Puttock — victim
- Jean "Jeannie" — Helen's sister; the key witness (appears as Langford, née Williams, later McLachlan)
- George Puttock — Helen's husband
- David and Michael — Helen's sons
- Det. Supt. Joe Beattie — led the 1969 inquiry
- Patricia Docker, Jemima MacDonald — the two earlier unresolved murders
- John Irvine McInnes — former suspect, **formally cleared** (1996)
- Barrowland Ballroom — the dance hall

# Never say

- Never name anyone as Bible John.
- Never say McInnes was Bible John or "got away with it". The Crown formally cleared him.
- Never say Peter Tobin was Bible John. Police found no evidence linking him.
- Never say it is proven that one man killed all three women. Say police linked them and many believe it.
- Never say the case is closed or solved.
- Never present the BBC podcast's cover-up claim as fact. It is an allegation police are assessing.
- Never say Helen was at a Halloween party. It was an ordinary Thursday dance.
- Never include menstruation, sanitary items, or bite-wound detail.
- Never give an exact time of death. It is not established in sources.
