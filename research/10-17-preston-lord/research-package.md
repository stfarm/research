## Case summary

- On **Saturday, October 28, 2023**, **Preston Lord**, 16, was at a **Halloween party** at a home in Queen Creek, a suburb in the East Valley of metro Phoenix. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S002]
- Outside the party he was beaten by a group. Queen Creek police arrived within about three minutes of the call and found him in the road. [10-17-S002]
- He died **two days later, on October 30, 2023**. The medical examiner ruled his death a **homicide** from multiple blunt-force injuries. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S007]
- After a four-month investigation, a grand jury indicted **seven young men** (ages about 17 to 20 at indictment) in March 2024. [10-17-S001, 10-17-S002]
- One, **William Owen Hines**, pleaded guilty to manslaughter and is serving 12 years for Preston's death. Six await trial. [10-17-S004, 10-17-S006]
- The case led to **"Preston's Law"** (HB 2611, signed May 12, 2025), making group "swarming" assaults a felony. [10-17-S013]

## Investigation

- **Response:** Officers arrived in about three minutes and found Preston in the roadway. [10-17-S002]
- **What police say led to it:** Police-report accounts describe a dispute after a gold chain was taken from one of Preston's friends. **This is the police/prosecution account, not a court finding.** [10-17-S002]
- **Evidence volume:** Prosecutors reviewed about **600 videos** and a **2,000-page** police report, and presented to an investigative grand jury over five weeks. [10-17-S001]
- **Criticism:** Families and the public criticised Queen Creek police for the four-month gap before arrests. [10-17-S002]
- **Context:** Media have linked some defendants to a loose group of East Valley teens dubbed the "Gilbert Goons" and to other assaults. This label is **media shorthand**, not a charge. Hines's own plea included two other 2022 group assaults. [10-17-S004, 10-17-S013]
- **Defence position (Meisner):** none of 13 interviewed eyewitnesses identified him by name or appearance. [10-17-S010]
