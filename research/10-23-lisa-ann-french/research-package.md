## Halloween connection

**Classification: A + B** — killed on **October 31** while **trick-or-treating** in costume.

- The Wisconsin Supreme Court opinion places the killing "on Halloween night, October 31, 1973". [10-23-S001]
- She was trick-or-treating alone, in a homemade **hobo costume**. [10-23-S006, 10-23-S002]
- Per S002 only: black felt hat, green parka, jeans with blue masking tape, freckles drawn on her cheeks; she was heading to an outdoor Halloween party at "Pumpkin Place" on East Bank Street after her friend was grounded. **Verify against 1973 Fond du Lac Reporter before using these specifics.**
- Aftermath: Fond du Lac today runs trick-or-treat in **daylight (3:30–5:30 p.m.) on the Sunday before Halloween**. WISN reports the schedule but does **not** say when it began or that it was adopted because of Lisa. Do not claim causation. [10-23-S005]

## Investigation

- Turner was an early suspect; S002 says a polygraph was inconclusive and he refused a second. [10-23-S002]
- **Physical evidence:** body hair and bedspread fibres from Turner were compared with samples from Lisa's body and clothing. [10-23-S001, 10-23-S002]
- **Confession** after about nine months (Aug 1974). At trial he said he confessed because he was "sick and tired of being harassed by police." [10-23-S006]
- Lead investigator: **Melvin Heller**, then a Fond du Lac police captain, later chief. "Everybody that lives here always knows about Lisa French." [10-23-S005]

## Story engine (angles, not script)

**Opening-hook angles (factual):**
1. A 9-year-old in a hobo costume goes trick-or-treating alone for the first time and never makes her 7 p.m. curfew. [S002, S006]
2. Five thousand people searched for Lisa. The man who took her lived down the street. [S006]
3. He has been out of prison three times since 1992 — and the state is still trying to keep him locked up in 2027. [S003]

**Suspense beats:** curfew passes → town-wide search with National Guard helicopters → a farmer spots a plastic bag on a fence → months of quiet suspicion of a neighbour → hair and bedspread fibres → confession nine months later.

**Reveal:** The killer was the neighbour whose door she knocked on (per his confession). Second reveal: second-degree verdict meant he walked out in 1992 — his release created Wisconsin's sexual-predator law.

**Unresolved question for the ending:** A jury will decide in January 2027 whether a 77-year-old Halloween Killer ever goes free. [S003]

## Distinguishing details

- One killer created a state law ("Turner's Law"). [S007, S008]
- The 1998 jury — the first big test of the law — found he was **not** a predator. [S007]
- Turner's line in a later letter: "On that night I became a monster." (S002 only — find the primary before quoting.)
- 2026 hook: the case is still in court 53 years later. [S003]

## Disputed claims, myths, facts to avoid

- **Never** say first-degree murder or "life sentence".
- **Never** say he is "committed" or "declared a sexual predator". He was found **not** one in 1998; the 2018 petition is undecided.
- **Never** say Fond du Lac "cancelled Halloween" or that daylight trick-or-treat was adopted "because of Lisa" — not sourced.
- **Do not** narrate the confession's account of the assault. Use "he lured her inside" only attributed: "he later confessed".
- **Do not** use "while on parole" (UPI 1998 only; uncorroborated).
- Some creators spell her name "Lisa Ann Frank" — wrong.
- Body-discovery date: Nov 3 (S002) vs "two days later" (People). Unresolved.
