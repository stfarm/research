# 10-19 — Quebec City Halloween sword attack (Old Quebec, 2020)

Research package. Not a script. Researched 2026-10-01. **Active legal matter: re-verify before publication.**

> ## LEGAL STATUS — READ FIRST (as of 2026-10-01)
> - **May 20, 2022:** a jury convicted Carl Girouard of 2 counts of first-degree murder and 5 of attempted murder. **June 10, 2022:** life, no parole for 25 years. [10-19-S002, 10-19-S003]
> - **March 31, 2026:** the Quebec Court of Appeal **set aside the convictions and sentence and ordered a new trial**, because the judge did not tell the jury it could not draw an inference of guilt from his silence in police interrogation. [10-19-S004, 10-19-S005]
> - **Late May 2026:** the Crown applied to the **Supreme Court of Canada** for leave to appeal, hoping to restore the verdict. Setting a new trial date was postponed to fall 2026. **No SCC decision found.** [10-19-S006, 10-19-S007]
> - **Today he is legally presumed innocent of murder.** He admitted carrying out the attacks; the live issue at trial was criminal responsibility (mental disorder). **Do not report the 2022 verdict or the 25-year sentence as current.**

## Case summary

- On **Halloween night, Saturday October 31, 2020**, beginning around **10:30 p.m.**, a man wearing a kimono-style costume and mask attacked people at random with a katana-like sword in **Old Quebec (Vieux-Québec)**, starting near **Le Château Frontenac** and the Parliament Building area. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S002, 10-19-S008]
- **Two people were killed**: **François Duchesne**, 56, and **Suzanne Clermont**, 61. **Five others were injured.** [10-19-S001]
- Police arrested **Carl Girouard**, 24, of Sainte-Thérèse (north of Montreal), shortly before 1 a.m. on November 1. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S008]
- Police said he had no criminal record and no terror links, but had described plans for this kind of act to a medical professional about five years earlier. [10-19-S001]
- At trial he admitted the attacks and argued he was not criminally responsible due to mental disorder. The jury rejected that defence and convicted him of first-degree murder (2022). [10-19-S003]
- In 2026 the Court of Appeal ordered a new trial. The Crown is seeking to reverse that at the Supreme Court of Canada. [10-19-S004, 10-19-S006]

## Victim profile

- **François Duchesne**, 56, a museum employee (which museum is not confirmed in sources reviewed). His sister **Marie-Josée Duchesne** spoke at sentencing of lasting sadness. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S002]
- **Suzanne Clermont**, 61, a hairdresser. Family member **Marie-Claude Veilleux** (sister-in-law) described her spouse's long need for therapy. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S002]
- **Five injured people.** Their identities were initially protected by a publication ban. **Do not name them.** [10-19-S001]
- Both victims were out in the city on a quiet pandemic-era evening; the attack was random. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S008]

## Investigation

- **Arrest:** within about 2.5 hours, after a city-wide alert; there was no identity dispute. [10-19-S001]
- **Evidence of planning:** Crown argued premeditation; he had talked about sword attacks with mental-health workers since his late teens. [10-19-S003]
- **Interrogation:** He stayed silent for hours during police questioning (Radio-Canada). At trial, witnesses and experts referred to his silence; the Court of Appeal held the jury needed a specific limiting instruction. [10-19-S005, 10-19-S011]
- **Expert battle:** defence psychiatrist Dr. Gilles Chamberland (autism spectrum disorder, schizophrenia, psychosis during the attack); Crown psychiatrist Dr. Sylvain Faucher (personality disorder; "narcissistic quest" for notoriety). [10-19-S003]
- **Failures:** No investigative failure alleged. Public debate focused on whether earlier warning signs (statements to clinicians) could have been acted on. [10-19-S001]

## Halloween connection

**Classification: A** (crime occurred October 31).

- The attacks began around 10:30 p.m. on **October 31, 2020** and continued past midnight. [10-19-S001]
- His costume was not linked to a Halloween event; in 2020, pandemic restrictions limited Halloween gatherings. Do not imply he targeted Halloween crowds. **Not B.**

## Story structure

- **Strongest factual hook:** Halloween 2020, a near-empty Old Quebec under pandemic rules. A man in a kimono and mask with a sword walks from the Château Frontenac through the old streets. In about two and a half hours, two people are dead and five injured. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S002]
- **Central question:** Not "who", but "was he criminally responsible?"
- **Turning point:** Two psychiatrists, two diagnoses; a jury rejects the mental-disorder defence. Then, four years later, an appeal court finds one missing jury instruction. [10-19-S003, 10-19-S004]
- **Ending (as of Oct 2026):** Verdict erased, new trial ordered, Crown asking the Supreme Court to reverse. Families wait again. [10-19-S005, 10-19-S006]
