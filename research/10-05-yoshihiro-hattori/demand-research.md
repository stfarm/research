## What the demand picture actually shows

1. **Creators do cover it**, steadily and specifically — ten videos located,
   nine of them single-case.
2. **The dominant packaging pattern is the Halloween hook plus the door**:
   "knocked on the wrong door," "wrong house Halloween," "wrong-door
   shooting." The door and the mistake, not the gun, are the hook.
3. **A recurring factual error in that packaging**: at least one summary opens
   with "Halloween night, 1992." That is wrong, and it is the differentiator —
   getting the date right while keeping the Halloween hook is available
   ground.
4. **Recognition is not creator-driven.** It rests on a published appellate
   opinion taught in law schools, a 1997 festival documentary, 1993 national
   newspaper coverage, a 2022 non-profit-newsroom feature and a university
   archive. That is a stronger fame base than YouTube counts would suggest.
5. **Angles other creators emphasise:** the wrong-door mistake; the word
   "Freeze"; the acquittal as outrage; US gun culture versus Japan. The
   **criminal/civil split**, which is the case's actual legal substance and is
   fully documented, appears mainly in the one law-education video (Y10) — it
   is under-served by true-crime creators.
6. **Absent from creator coverage entirely:** the 2022 developments (the
   Hattoris' retirement from advocacy; Webb Haymaker's death). Newest factual
   material available.
