## What the demand picture actually shows

1. **Creators cover this case consistently, and it travels internationally** —
   seven independent videos located including two in German, plus a syndicated
   TV segment and a *Dateline* hour.
2. **Packaging is overwhelmingly Halloween-forward**: "Halloween Horror,"
   "Halloween Party Mystery," "Missing from a Halloween Party,"
   "Halloween-Tragödie." Every treatment leads with Halloween. **None of the
   titles surveyed claims 31 October** — the honest framing is already the
   convention here, which removes the usual tension between accuracy and
   packaging.
3. **A distinct competing format exists:** interrogation-footage videos (Y04,
   and the UNILAD write-up of the same material). That angle is well covered,
   and it is also the angle most likely to slide into treating his account as
   fact. Worth deliberately not competing on.
4. **Mainstream recognition is real and independent of YouTube:** *Dateline*
   with Keith Morrison, *Crime Watch Daily*, ABC/NBC/CBS national coverage in
   both 2014 and 2017, and sustained *Detroit News* and *Toledo Blade*
   reporting.
5. **Angles other creators emphasise:** the vanishing from a crowded party; the
   costume; the interrogation; "the killer realises he's caught."
6. **Under-served and available:** the **legal mechanism** — that a 2015
   Michigan arrest-DNA law, enacted after she died, is what produced the CODIS
   hit; the **geographic separation** of costume and body across a county line;
   and the **appellate record** (affirmed 2019, leave denied 2020), which no
   surveyed creator treatment appears to reach.
7. **Most recent factual development available:** the Michigan Supreme Court's
   denial of leave on 3 March 2020. Nothing later was located.
