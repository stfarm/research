# Script facts — 10-19 Quebec City Halloween sword attack

Verified facts only. Source IDs refer to `source-log.md`. **Status as of 2026-10-01. Re-check the Supreme Court of Canada leave decision before publishing.**

# Essential facts

- **Saturday, October 31, 2020, from about 10:30 p.m.**, Old Quebec (Vieux-Québec), Quebec City. [10-19-S001]
- A man in a short kimono, black mask and boots, carrying a katana-style sword, attacked people at random, starting near **Le Château Frontenac**. [10-19-S001, 10-19-S002]
- **François Duchesne**, 56, a museum employee, and **Suzanne Clermont**, 61, a hairdresser, were killed. **Five others were injured.** [10-19-S001]
- Police counted about 25 crime scenes. [10-19-S001]
- **Carl Girouard**, 24, from Sainte-Thérèse near Montreal, was arrested shortly before 1 a.m. [10-19-S001]
- Police said he had no record and no terror links, but had described plans for this kind of attack in a medical setting about five years earlier. [10-19-S001]

# Timeline

- Nov 1, 2020: charged with two counts of first-degree murder and five of attempted murder. [10-19-S001]
- 2022 trial: he admitted the attacks but said he was not criminally responsible because of mental illness. [10-19-S003]
- **May 20, 2022**: the jury rejected that defence and convicted him on all counts. [10-19-S003]
- **Jun 10, 2022**: life, no parole for 25 years. [10-19-S002]
- **Mar 31, 2026**: the Quebec Court of Appeal threw out the verdict and ordered a new trial. [10-19-S004, 10-19-S005]
- Late May 2026: prosecutors asked the Supreme Court of Canada to restore the verdict. The new trial is on hold. [10-19-S006, 10-19-S007]

# Evidence reveal

- The trial was not about who did it; he admitted it. It was about whether he was criminally responsible. [10-19-S003]
- Defence psychiatrist: autism spectrum disorder, schizophrenia, psychosis during the attack. Crown psychiatrist: a personality disorder and a "narcissistic quest" for notoriety. [10-19-S003]
- He testified that since about age 18 he had a "mission" involving killing people with a sword. [10-19-S003]
- He stayed silent for hours in police interrogation. The appeal court said the jury should have been told clearly that his silence could not be used as evidence of guilt. [10-19-S004, 10-19-S011]

# Ending

- **Current status:** the 2022 conviction and sentence have been set aside. He is legally presumed innocent and faces a new trial, unless the Supreme Court of Canada agrees to hear the Crown's appeal and restores the verdict. [10-19-S005, 10-19-S006]
- His lawyer after the appeal ruling: "On repart à zéro" ("We're starting from zero"). [10-19-S005]
- Families of both victims described lasting grief at the 2022 sentencing. [10-19-S002]

# Never say

- Never say he is serving a life sentence or is a convicted murderer as of now. The verdict was set aside on March 31, 2026.
- Never say the appeal court found him innocent or that he didn't do it. It ordered a new trial over a jury instruction.
- Never say the Supreme Court has ruled. As of Oct 1, 2026, the Crown's leave application is pending.
- Never name the five injured survivors.
- Never call him a "medieval knight" or describe him as dressed in armour. Trial evidence describes a kimono and mask.
- Never say he attacked Halloween partiers. The streets were quiet under pandemic rules.
- Never state his custody status without confirming it.
- Never offer a diagnosis. Report only what each expert said at trial.
